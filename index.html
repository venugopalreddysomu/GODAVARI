How a threshold gets chosen. The ADXL367 wakes this node when the deck moves more than a set amount away from how it normally sits — gravity and bracket tilt are removed in hardware, so the number below is a deviation, not an absolute acceleration. Profile the quiet bridge, profile a train, and the separation between the two peaks is what the threshold sits in.

Runs for the configured window with no train on the span. Learns the site’s resting orientation, stores it, and measures the idle noise floor. Required before the node will arm.

The ADXL367 waits for an inactivity event after each train. If the deck never settles below the quiet threshold — wind, road traffic, a following train — it stays asserted and no further train produces an interrupt. The node checks its own wake path on this interval and re-arms if it finds that state, or if the part’s registers have drifted. It never runs during a capture or an upload.

Stress per kN is the setting that turns fatigue on. It converts measured load into the stress range at the monitored detail, and it can only come from the structural engineer for that specific member and connection — the firmware has no way to derive it. While it is 0 the node still accumulates damage so the pipeline is exercised, but it reports UNCALIBRATED and the figure has no physical meaning.

Cumulative fatigue — rainflow cycles by load range

Clearing is permanent and cannot be undone from here. Accumulated damage is the running total of every train this node has ever weighed, and it is the one figure on the device that nothing else can reconstruct — everything else is either a live reading or recoverable from the cloud. Clear it when the node is commissioned onto the structure, so bench trials do not count, or when it moves to a different member. The S-N curve, the calibration, the queued events and the lifetime counters are all kept.

Takes around 15 seconds and leaves the modem powered so you can swap the SIM or move the antenna and run it again. Power it off when you are finished — it is the largest draw on the board.
CSQ is not the number that decides. On LTE it is a wideband reading that includes interference, so it can look healthy while the serving cell is unusable. RSRP is the one to judge by: better than −95 dBm is good, below −110 dBm will struggle.

Tilt is only meaningful while the node is still. The angles come from the direction of gravity, so during a train — or with a hand on the enclosure — they measure the deck's motion instead and are refused rather than shown.

Judge by the change, not the absolute. The part's zero-g offset puts about a degree of error on the absolute angles, but it is the same error every time, so it cancels almost entirely in the difference: a 0.3° change since commissioning is real, while a 0.3° absolute reading is not. A drift here with no change in load is a bracket loosening or a bearing rotating.

Every angle here is an average of 32 samples, not a single reading. The part carries about 2 mg of broadband noise, which near level is 0.13° — enough that a motionless node appears to wander by a quarter of a degree. Averaging 32 divides that by √32, giving about 0.02°. The Noise row is the spread seen while averaging: around 2 mg is the sensor itself, and much more than that means something is physically shaking.
A part lying still should read about 1000 mg magnitude — that is gravity, and it is the quickest proof the part is alive, level and scaled correctly.
